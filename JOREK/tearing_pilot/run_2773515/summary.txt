NUMERICAL LIMITS
Endpoint agreement alone is insufficient. Half/quarter trajectory RMS differences, normalized by the finest-run temporal standard deviation:
rho=0.11%, T=0.23%, psi=4.62%, u=60.34%, j=109.30%, omega=6.74%.
Therefore u and j transients remain sensitive to timestep despite much better final-state agreement.
No mesh refinement, higher toroidal-mode convergence, energy balance, or native constraint residual validation was performed.
Crank-Nicholson integration and the stored restart history warrant investigation before interpreting fast fluctuations as learnable physics.
Quadrature q4/q8 RMS differences are below 1.4e-5 relative. Reconstructed volume and density integral match native diagnostics to ~1e-12 and ~2e-13.
Two analytic interpolation/norm checks pass. Source and binary snapshots and input hashes are preserved.

IMPLICATIONS FOR OPSSPLIT VERSUS AR/NODE
Use long-rollout accuracy, stability, and sample efficiency as primary hypotheses; do not assume OpsSplit wins.
One-step scalar prediction is easy here: baseline linear extrapolation errors are 2.06% (rho) and 1.55% (T) of temporal standard deviation.
At ten saved steps (~19.45 ms), those errors rise to 77.8% and 36.0%. Persistence errors exceed 100% for both.
First resolve u/current transient convergence and check spatial resolution. Audit model199 terms and time discretization before composing operators.
Then generate independent initial-condition families and modest transport-coefficient variations, with sufficient field activity.
All current branches share one parent state: do not put them in different train/test splits.
Use per-field and perturbation-aware scaling so static flux/current backgrounds cannot dominate the score.
Give AR, NODE, and OpsSplit identical observed states, coefficient inputs, splits, training budgets, and rollout horizons; include capacity-matched controls.
Keep simple persistence/extrapolation baselines. Report failures, physical constraints and perturbation errors alongside full-field errors.
